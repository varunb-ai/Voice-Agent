The Refusal Loop

The patient does not sound robotic because of its voice. Measured against the human receptionist on the same line, the agent is the more prosodically varied speaker. It sounds robotic because the guard layer keeps telling it the receptionist never answered — and the only move it has left is to ask again.

01

Executive summary

Why does this agent sound monotonic, robotic and scripted despite a green suite?

Because three grounding guards throw away real answers, the agent is told the receptionist said nothing, and it re-asks. The suite is green because it is a regression corpus built from this project's own calls — every fixture is a phrasing this system has already seen, from one office of colleagues who answer in one word.

The investigation traced the full path from Twilio media in to Twilio media out, measured the delivered waveform against the human on the same call, and audited every save refusal in 51 September calls. Three things came back clearly.

3.71 st

Against the human receptionist's 2.61 st on the same recording. The voice is not flat. Holds across 28 calls.

69% of calls carry at least one. 56 of 79 are on save_doctor_identity — the first question asked.

The one-paragraph mechanism

A US receptionist answers the phone with a greeting in front of the answer: "Good afternoon, yes, Dr. Abel is one of our pediatricians here." classify_identity anchors its affirmative at ^\W*(yes|yeah|yep|yup) — string start — so the greeting defeats it, and the named-subject form ("Dr. Abel is one of our…") is not in the pattern at all. The guard returns None, the save is refused, and the refusal text goes back to the model saying nothing the caller said since you asked reads as that answer. The model retries, is refused again, and eventually does the only thing left: it re-asks a question that was already answered. The receptionist hears a machine. Everything downstream — the stacked questions, the narration, the dead air, the backwards walk through the objective — is this loop plus the repairs bolted on top of it.

The most important correction to the current mental model

Previous work treated flat delivery as a prompt-tone problem and spent a token-ceiling raise on it. The measurement says the tone work succeeded and was never the bottleneck: marin delivers more pitch variation than the human it is talking to, on every call in the corpus. Continuing to tune the persona will not move the complaint.

Voice & prosody — measured, not assumed

F0 by autocorrelation, 40 ms frames / 10 ms hop, voiced frames only, median-filtered for octave errors. Pitch dynamism is reported in semitones — the standard perceptual unit, and the only one not confounded by speaker mean. Below ~1.5 st reads as monotone; ordinary engaged conversation sits around 2.5–4 st.

The comparison is controlled the same way the outbound-conditioning work was: agent against the human on the same recording, same codec, same line, same instrument. Channel assignment was established empirically — matching segment onsets and words-per-minute against the transcript, then confirmed by f0 (marin renders at 200 Hz offline and measures 208 Hz live).

The falsifiable claim, and it survived

If the complaint were acoustic, the agent's pitch dynamism would sit below the human's. It sits 42% above it, on the failing call and across the corpus. Voice selection, Realtime voice config, resampling, μ-law and the 8 kHz band are all excluded as causes of "monotonic".

What the measurement does find

Energy is genuinely flatter, and identical every call

Agent energy SD lands at 4.5–5.2 dB on every single call; humans scatter 5.0–9.2 dB. That is the 3:1 compressor doing exactly what it was built to do for intelligibility. It costs ~1.7 dB of loudness variation, and — the more interesting half — it removes the call-to-call variation a person has. Real but second-order.

The persona instruction measurably flattens pitch

From the existing A/B renders, same line, only delivery varying:

marin A-flat   3.85 / 3.93 / 4.51 st
marin B-humble  2.90 / 3.23 / 2.24 st

"Subtly under the weather" removes 25–40% of pitch dynamism. It still lands inside the normal band, so it is not the complaint — but the headroom is real if you want it back.

The chain is prosody-transparent, and it is transparent by construction, not by luck:

  • A linear filter cannot move F0. Decimation to 8 kHz cannot either — the whole 70–350 Hz F0 range sits an octave and more below Nyquist.
  • The compressor acts on level, with 5 ms attack and 80 ms release. It reduces crest by ~4 dB and leaves timing, pitch and pause structure untouched.
  • The seam defect the class was written to fix is genuinely fixed: the suite asserts chunked output is byte-identical to one-shot, including ragged chunk sizes.

Control tests also rule out the transport as a measurement artefact: applying a 300 Hz PSTN high-pass and a 22.05 kHz MP3 rate round-trip to a known render moved f0 by 5 Hz and pitch SD by 0.08 st. Nothing in decoding, conversion, filtering, normalisation, chunking or buffering is flattening dynamics, pitch, timing or transients.

One real audio finding, and it is about silence, not spectrum

43% of the failing call is silence with neither party speaking. That is the audible "robotic" texture at the waveform level — not the timbre of the voice, but the shape of the gaps between turns. See Latency.

Is the model over-prompted? Yes — but the static prompt is the smaller half

6,927 tokens of instruction with 234 prohibitions is a lot, and the "one ask / answer and ask / not every turn" cluster is a genuine source of the two-item defect. But the sharper problem is where the workflow instructions arrive.

All ~28 mid-call directives are injected as role:"user" messages whose text begins (system: …). From the model's side, the conversation reads like this:

user "Good afternoon, yes, Dr. Abel is one of our pediatrician here." assistant [function_call save_doctor_identity] tool "NOT SAVED — identity='confirmed' — nothing the caller said since you asked reads as that answer | RE-READ: their turns" assistant [function_call save_doctor_identity] ×5 user "(system: you just gave one of your own details with nothing in front of it … Tell them now, in one short sentence …)" assistant "Okay, I'm not registered with you yet, and I'll wait while…"

The user channel is where the receptionist lives. Filling it with workflow commands does three things at once: it breaks the turn-taking rhythm the model has a strong prior for, it establishes in-context that turns can be instructions, and it makes the next assistant turn a reply to a workflow command. That reply comes out in workflow register — "let me think about how to answer that", "I'll wait while you check", "could you just say it plainly for me". All three of the call's most robotic lines follow a refusal or an injected directive.

07

Conversation state & the objective system

Does the objective system prioritise database completion over natural conversation?

Not by design — the budgets, gates and deferrals are sound. It happens because a field the guards have made uncollectable stays "owed" forever, and an owed field is indistinguishable from an unasked one.

Turn 5 is the whole story. The caller named the clinic; the transcriber rendered it "notary clinic"; the model heard the audio and wrote "North Clinic"; grounding read the transcript and contradicted it. From that moment branch_confirmed could never be satisfied, because the only evidence that could satisfy it was a transcript line that does not contain the words.

Everything the user read as "the objective forcing the conversation backwards" follows mechanically:

  • Turn 10 — the branch ask is stapled onto the DOB answer because the field is owed.
  • Turn 18 — after the waitlist question is fully answered, the only outstanding field is the branch, so the agent returns to it. Failure E is the objective working exactly as specified against a field that cannot be collected.
  • Turn 20 — premature_ending_label refuses no_capacity because a required field is still owed. Correct guard, unreachable precondition.

The ask budget did not misfire: ask_budget.verdict = "not needed", directive_sent: false. The give-up path never armed. This is not a budget problem.

The evidence-window ceiling is a second false-negative

_ungrounded_choice bounds its window above as well as below: an ask for any other field closes the current field's window. That ceiling was added for a real defect (an identity confirmed at turn 2 being overwritten by a branch answer eight turns later) and it is correct for that case.

It assumes callers answer questions in the order asked. Real callers defer. Here the receptionist said "Yeah, before that, I need to ask one information", answered a name and a date of birth, and only then came back to new patients — by which time the agent's own branch ask had closed the window. The guard's own message is precise about what it did: THEY SAID: 'Yeah, before that, I need to ask one information.'

08

Turn generation & the second spoken item

The hold-and-release machinery is not masking the root cause, and it should not be removed. It is doing the job it was built for: on this call it correctly discarded two verbatim duplicates and correctly released two items carrying real substance. Removing it would put the call back to the state where the model split a retry four times and the caller heard four filler intros and never the question.

But it has two costs that were not visible before, and both are real:

① Release manufactures stapled turns

A released item is played immediately after the first, so the caller hears one long turn containing two separate moves. Turns 10 and 14 are both this. Corpus-wide: 33 second-item events across 417 agent turns (7.9%), and 44 stapled questions across 224 question turns (19.6%). Some of that staple rate is manufactured downstream of generation, not produced by the model as one turn.

② Release corrupts every "previous agent turn" guard

A released item calls sess.add_turn("agent", …) in its own right — deliberately, so the turn guards see it. The side effect is that _prev_agent for the second item is the model's own first item. That is exactly how _detail_left_bare lost sight of a disclaimer standing one caller-turn back and fired the directive that produced Failure C.

Why the model emits two items in the first place

Three of the four second items on this call were a filler-only first item plus the substance. The prompt's turn-shape section explicitly bans that split — "Wrong: 'Got it.' (reacted, told them nothing, they wait)" — and asks for reaction and ask "folded into ONE sentence". The model complies with the intent by producing both, and disagrees with the API about how many items that is.

The reliable trigger is the moment the model must satisfy several turn-shape rules at once: answer them first, one ask per turn, never end with nothing, don't repeat, don't staple. Turn 10 is the maximum of that function, and it is the turn that produced both the banned filler and a second item. This is the best available evidence that the turn-shape rule cluster — not prompt length as such — is what drives the split.

Barge-in & interruption

Currently healthy, and recently fixed. 32 drain barge-ins across 51 calls (~0.6/call). The failing call recorded zero, and no rejection_cancels, no cancelled-by-OpenAI events, no repair prompts fired anywhere in the corpus.

The artifacts do show the earlier defect and its fix. From call-20260903-2231 onward the floor is 0.060 with a 300 ms voiced-run requirement, and the two events on that call measure 0.2057 and 0.1497 — unambiguous speech.

Backchannels

Armed and firing: 20 calls carried 1–9 clips each after the 2026-09-03 pause-detection rewrite, with backchannel_echo_frames recorded alongside. They are working as specified.

On the failing call zero fired, correctly. _BACKCHANNEL_AFTER_S is 6.5 s and the receptionist's longest single turn was 5.8 s of speech. The gate is behaving; the call simply had no qualifying turn.

Which is the finding worth recording: backchannels are structurally unable to help this failure mode. They fire inside a long caller turn. They are neither masking a turn-taking problem nor solving one; treat them as unrelated to the complaint.

Root-cause ranking

P0

classify_identity rejects the way Americans answer a phone

Root cause
The CONFIRMED pattern anchors its affirmative at string start (^\W*(yes|yeah|yep|yup)\b) and accepts a presence claim only with a pronoun subject ((he|she|they) (works?|practi[cs]es?) here). A greeting before the "yes" defeats it, and the named-subject form — "Dr. Abel is one of our pediatricians here" — is not in the pattern at all. Notably, not (one of )?our is in the NOT_HERE pattern; only the positive was never written.
Evidence

Running the shipped function over 26 realistic front-desk confirmations: 17 miss (65%). Every miss is a greeting prefix, a named subject, or "one of our…".

56 of 79 refusals in the corpus are on save_doctor_identity; 36 are the classifier returning no-answer and 10 more are the window then reading as questions-only. Six separate calls contain the same refused sentence shape: "Hello, yes, Dr. X is one of our physicians here. How can I help you today?"

On the failing call it produced 5 refusals, 9.75 s of inference_2, 12.14 s of silence, and Failure A's re-ask.

Files
agents/voice/objectives.py — _IDENTITY_PATTERNS, the IdentityAnswer.CONFIRMED entry (~line 570) and classify_identity.
Why it sounds robotic
The model is told, in its own context, that a question it heard answered was not answered. The prompt simultaneously forbids re-asking ("if you heard them fine, do NOT ask again") and forbids telling the caller they did not say something. The only behaviour that satisfies the tool and the prompt is a hedged re-ask — "could you just say it plainly for me" — which is precisely the register a person never uses.
Recommended fix
Extend the existing _LEAD_IN idea to cover greetings, and add the named-subject and "one of our" forms. A candidate was written and tested during this investigation: positives go 56% → 97% (31/32) with 0 of 18 negatives broken, including five adversarial greeting-prefixed denials ("Good morning, no, Dr. Abel doesn't work here." still lands NOT_HERE). The existing ordering — WRONG_NUMBER, UNSURE, NOT_HERE, then CONFIRMED — and _negated_before are what make the loosening safe, and both are untouched.
Risk
Low, and quantified rather than asserted. The change loosens only the lead-in and adds two positive presence forms; it cannot outrank a denial because CONFIRMED is checked last.
P0

A mangled surname is read as a different doctor

Root cause
_wrong_doctor_named compares the transcriber's rendering of the surname against ours. The transcriber mangles surnames routinely, and every mangling reads as the receptionist naming somebody else.
Evidence
17 refusals — 22% of all refusals — on: abul, okofor, rokofor, pokofor, okopher, april, brown, walcott, and once the word here. On the failing call, name_mismatches records heard:"ables" / ours:"abel" with passive:true. These are one edit away from ours, in a sentence that otherwise confirms the doctor.
Files
agents/voice/evidence/names.py, called from _ungrounded_identity in agents/voice/realtime_worker.py.
Why it sounds robotic
It forces the spell-and-confirm repair on calls where nothing was ambiguous, and blocks identity — which gates every other field.
Recommended fix
Do not add fuzzy matching — the existing note is right that no threshold separates Riaz→Reyes from Kapoor→Okafor. Instead narrow the trigger: fire _wrong_doctor_named only when the named surname is contrastive — the sentence is a correction or an offer of an alternative — rather than on any near-miss token appearing beside a confirmation. A sentence that both names a doctor and affirms presence is evidence for our doctor with a bad transcript, not evidence of a different one.
Risk
Medium. This guard prevents the highest-severity defect in the project — a row confirmed against the wrong person. Narrow it with a corpus built from the 17 observed cases plus the genuine wrong-doctor cases already in the suite, and keep the passive/active distinction that name_mismatches already records.

The evidence-window ceiling discards a deferred answer

Root cause
In _ungrounded_choice, an ask for any other field sets until and closes the current field's window. It assumes callers answer in the order asked.
Evidence
Turn 13's clear no was refused twice quoting THEY SAID: 'Yeah, before that, I need to ask one information.' — the window ran turns 7–9 and the answer was at 13. Corpus-wide, 10 refusals land in the questions-only branch.
Files
agents/voice/evidence/guards.py — _ungrounded_choice, the until loop (~line 496–522).

Keep the ceiling; make it reopenable. P1

The tool round trip puts a second inference on the critical path

Root cause
The model emits a function_call, we run the tool, send function_call_output, wait for response.done, and only then response.create for speech. The caller hears nothing until inference 2 completes.
Evidence
Stage data, same call: tool turns felt 2.26 / 2.78 / 4.06 / 5.81 / 11.27 s; no-tool turns felt 1.13 / 1.16 / 1.18 / 1.24 s. our_work is 0.00 s on every stage, and deferral 0.00–0.03 s — the gap is entirely the extra inference.
Files
agents/voice/lifecycle.py::_handle_response_done (the _pending_response_create branch), agents/voice/grounding/teardown.py.
Why it sounds robotic
It makes the pause length correlate with how substantive the caller's turn was. The receptionist says something important and the patient goes quiet for four seconds. That is the single most machine-like timing signature available.
Recommended fix
Teach the prompt to acknowledge out loud in the same response as the tool call, so the caller hears the reaction after inference 1 and the substantive follow-up after inference 2. The Realtime API emits multiple output items per response and a function_call item produces no audio deltas, so this does not collide with the second-item mute. This is a re-ordering, not new machinery — and it should be measured, not assumed, on live calls.
Risk
Medium. Interacts with the second-item hold and with _close_deferred. Do it after the P0 classifier fixes, which remove most of the retries that make this leg long.
P1

Released second items corrupt every "previous agent turn" guard

Root cause
A released item calls sess.add_turn("agent", …) as an independent turn, so _prev_agent for it is the model's own first item and _callers_since resets to 0.
Evidence
bare_pii_details on the failing call records prev_agent: "Okay, let me think about how to answer that.", callers_since: 0. The real previous agent turn carried the disclaimer one caller-turn earlier. The model followed CALL CONTEXT's one-turn exception exactly and the guard punished it for it.
Files
agents/voice/turns.py ~2159–2175 (the _prev_agent/_callers_since walk) and ~2013–2057 (the release site).
Why it sounds robotic
Produced Failure C directly: a spurious safety directive fired, and its repair landed on a hold, giving "I'm not registered with you yet, and I'll wait while you check."
Recommended fix
Mark the released item as a continuation of the same response (a flag on the turn, or record the response id), and have the _prev_agent walk skip turns from the response currently being judged. The item must stay a real turn — the turn guards need to see what the caller heard — so the fix is in the walk, not in whether it is added.
Risk
Low, if scoped to the walk. Several guards read _prev_agent; fix it once, in one place, and assert all of them agree.

Low. Editing example text, not rules. Do not rewrite the prompt wholesale.
P2

Root cause
~28 injection sites send machine text as role:"user" items. The channel that carries the receptionist also carries the workflow.

3:1 with +8.17 dB makeup costs ~1.7 dB of loudness variation against the human, and makes every call measure the same. Deliberate, evidence-based, and correct for intelligibility. Revisit only after everything above.

Backchannels
Working as specified, structurally unrelated to this failure mode.

Healthy; the echo-floor defect was fixed on 2026-09-03 and the fix is visible in the artifacts.

What not to change

The second-item hold-and-release

It correctly discarded two duplicates and released two substantive halves on this call alone. Fix its side effect on _prev_agent; keep the machinery.

Objective tracking and the two-counter budget

The budget never armed on this call (verdict: "not needed"). The objective walked backwards because a field was uncollectable, not because the tracking is wrong.

The ending-label refusal and the deferred-save hold

Both behaved exactly as designed. They are downstream symptoms of the branch defect — debugging either finds nothing, which is the lesson the zombie-goodbye cascade already bought.

Outbound conditioning

Measured prosody-transparent here, and it fixed a real intelligibility deficit and a real chunk-seam defect. Leave realtime_output_format = "pcm" alone.

The grounding architecture itself

The principle — never save what the caller did not say — is right and is why this project has no fabricated rows. The defect is that three specific predicates are too narrow, not that evidence should be optional.

VAD, echo gate, drain floor, backchannels

All measured healthy. The 0.060 drain floor is doing its job; leave it.

Test plan & live validation

Why the suite is green — and the one change that stops it happening again

Every positive fixture for classify_identity begins with the affirmative token or is the bare phone idiom "Speaking": "Yes, this is Dr. Okafor's office.", "Yeah, it is.", "Yeah, this is, yeah, that's right, Dr. Kapoor is one of our cardiologists.", "Yes?", "Speaking.". Not one carries a greeting in front. The suite is a regression corpus harvested from this project's own calls, and every call so far has been a Hyderabad colleague who knows it is a test and answers in one word. 2,556 checks cannot fail on a phrasing the system has never been shown.

So the fix that matters most is not a fix at all: the corpus needs a population it did not generate.

Tests needed

  1. A held-out phrasing corpus for every closed-set classifier, written from how US front desks actually answer rather than from what previous calls produced. Minimum: greeting-prefixed affirmatives, named-subject presence claims, "one of our…", trailing "how can I help you", and the matching greeting-prefixed denials as negatives. Track a miss rate as a number, and assert a ceiling on it — the same discipline the token ceiling already applies to the prompt.
  2. Mutation-test each fix. Revert it, confirm the new checks fail, restore. The P0 candidate was already validated this way during the investigation: 31/32 positives, 0/18 negatives broken.
  3. A false-negative assertion for the window ceiling: a caller who defers ("before that, I need to ask…"), the agent asks another field, the caller then answers the first — assert the save is accepted. This class of test does not currently exist anywhere.
  4. _prev_agent across a released second item: assert the walk reaches the previous real agent turn, and that _detail_left_bare stays silent when a disclaimer is standing one caller-turn back.
  5. Split the 11,354-line main() so pyright stops reporting "Code is too complex to analyze". A function pyright has given up on is one where nothing inside is checked — it has already hidden one real error.

Live call validation

Tests cannot settle this. The success criterion is a call that sounds like two people, so validate on calls and read the recordings, not the artifacts.

16

Final diagnosis

The patient agent sounds monotonic, robotic and scripted not because of anything in the audio path and not because the model is a poor speaker. Measured against the human on the other end of the same call, the agent carries 42% more pitch variation, and the outbound conditioner alters pitch by 0.01 semitones end to end.

It sounds robotic because of a loop. A US receptionist gives a normal, correct, greeting-prefixed answer. Three grounding predicates — an affirmative anchored to string start, a name check that reads a transcription error as a different doctor, and an evidence window that closes when a caller defers — report that no answer was given. The refusal text goes back into the model's context. The model retries, costing a whole extra inference each time, and then does the only thing available: it asks again. The receptionist answers again. The record still cannot be written, so the objective keeps the field owed, the agent keeps returning to it, the repair directives fire, and each repair lands in the user channel where the receptionist's own words live.

Every symptom in the brief is a station on that loop. The repeated question is the refusal. The narration is a repair directive being obeyed. The stacked questions are a held second item being released. The dead air is the extra inference the retries bought. The backwards walk through the objective is a field the guards made uncollectable.

And the suite is green throughout, because it is a regression corpus grown from this project's own calls, and this project has only ever called colleagues who answer in one word.

The fix is not to loosen the safety principle. It is to make three narrow predicates recognise the language real receptionists speak — which the tested candidate does at 97% with nothing broken — and then to stop making the caller wait through a second inference for every fact the agent writes down.
